# Chapter 1: The Girl in the Apartment

Suddenly, I found out that I was engaged.

The problem?

I did not remember getting engaged.

Not the proposal.

Not the ring.

Not even the person I was apparently going to marry.

All I had was an apartment address sent to my phone and a message by mom telling me to go there.

That was it.

Honestly, whoever had designed my life had a strange sense of humour.

I stood outside the apartment building for a few seconds, staring at the address on my phone.

*Third floor.*

I looked up.

Three floors.

Simple enough.

I walked inside and started climbing the stairs.

One floor.

Two floors.

Three—

I stopped.

*Wait.*

I looked around.

*Was it the third floor or the fourth?*

I frowned at my phone.

Nothing.

I climbed another flight before finally noticing the number written clearly beside the door.

**3.**  

I stared at it.

Then at the stairs behind me.

Then back at the number.

*...Right.*

Apparently, my brain was still recovering from whatever disaster had happened last night.

I went back down.

The apartment door was slightly open.

I stopped again.

That felt suspicious.

I slowly pushed the door.

Then remembered something very important.

*Right. Knock first.*

I knocked.

Nothing.

I knocked again.

Still nothing.

> "Hello?"

Silence.

I waited for a few seconds.

> "Anyone here?"

A faint voice came from somewhere inside.

> "Come in..."

I froze.

That voice.

Soft.

Sweet.

And for some completely unreasonable reason, my heart suddenly decided to beat twice as fast.

What was this?

I had barely heard two words.

And yet my brain had already started making unnecessary observations.

*Nice voice.*

I immediately rejected the thought.

*Stop.*

I opened the door and stepped inside.

The apartment was surprisingly neat.

Not the artificial kind of neat where everything looked untouched.

It felt lived in.

There were a few books on the table, a small plant near the window and a laptop bag placed carefully beside the sofa.

Then I saw her.

She was standing near the dining table, fixing something on the counter.

She turned toward me.

And smiled.

> "Oh, you came."

My heart fluttered again.

*Oh.*

I maintained a completely straight face.

Inside?

Absolute chaos.

She wiped her hands and walked toward me.

> "Your luggage can stay here for now. We'll decide where everything goes later."

Then she pointed toward the dining table.

> "And you should eat this before it gets cold."

I looked at the food.

Then at her.

> "You made this?"

She blinked.

*Of course.*

She said it as if that was the most obvious thing in the world.

I sat down.

She pulled out the chair opposite me.

> "You look confused."

> "I am."

That came out more honestly than I intended.

She smiled.

> "I figured."

I picked up the spoon.

For some reason, I suddenly felt nervous.

*This was ridiculous.*

I had written code while simultaneously handling three different tasks at work without losing track of any of them.

I had debugged production issues at two in the morning.

I had presented technical solutions to people who barely understood the problem.

Yet sitting in front of this girl with a plate of homemade food made me feel like I had forgotten how humans were supposed to behave.

I took the first bite.

...

I stopped.

She noticed.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "You made a face."

> "I didn't."

> "You did."

I looked down at my plate.

> "It's good."

Her expression softened.

> "Really?"

I nodded.

> "Really."

She smiled again.

That smile was becoming dangerous.

I continued eating quietly.

Then she casually said,

> "So... the wedding is in two weeks."

My spoon stopped halfway to my mouth.

I slowly looked at her.

> "Two weeks?"

She nodded.

> "Yes."

*Oh.*

The world had confirmed it again.

This wasn't some strange misunderstanding.

She was actually going to be my wife.

My heart suddenly felt warm.

*My wife.*

The words themselves felt strangely natural.

Almost too natural.

I looked down again before she could notice anything.

> "Yes."

> "That's all?"

> "What else should I say?"

She laughed softly.

> "I don't know. Most people would probably panic."

> "I am not panicking."

She raised an eyebrow.

> "You've been holding that spoon in the same position for ten seconds."

I looked at my hand.

It was, in fact, still holding the spoon.

I immediately started eating again.

She smiled to herself.

I pretended not to notice.

After breakfast, she stood up and picked up her bag.

> "I have to leave for work."

> "Already?"

She nodded.

> "Consulting doesn't really believe in leaving people alone."

I almost smiled.

She worked for a consulting firm.

I already knew that.

Well...

Technically, I knew a lot of things about her.

Her company.

Her position.

Her general work profile.

The fact that she was considered exceptionally good at what she did.

The fact that her salary was almost similar to mine.

The fact that she was confident enough to walk into a room full of executives and make everyone listen.

But somehow...

I knew almost nothing about what she actually liked.

That thought stayed in the back of my mind.

She picked up her bag.

> "If you need anything, call me once. I'll explain everything about the apartment."

> "I'll be fine."

She looked at me.

> "Are you sure?"

> "Yeah. I'll also leave for work after some time."

She nodded.

> "Okay. Then we'll figure everything out tonight."

> "Sure."

She smiled.

> "See you later."

> "See you."

She turned around.

Then stopped.

*Wait.*

I looked up.

She looked at me for a second.

> "You're not going to ask me where anything is?"

> "No."

> "Why?"

> "I'll figure it out."

She stared at me.

Then smiled.

> "Of course you will."

Something about the way she said it made me feel like she was laughing at me without actually laughing.

She walked toward the door.

Just before leaving, she turned back once.

> "Don't forget your lunch."

I nodded.

She left.

The door closed.

Silence.

I stared at it.

...

Then I leaned back in the chair.

> "Man."

I covered my face with one hand.

*What is happening?*

I had just met my future wife.

Apparently.

And she had cooked breakfast for me.

She had smiled at me.

She had blushed once.

At least...

I thought she had blushed.

Was she actually blushing?

I sat there for a moment.

Then smiled.

*Man, I'm getting married.*

The thought felt unreal.

Yet somehow...

Good.

Very good.

I checked the time.

I still had around an hour and a half before leaving for work.

I looked toward the door.

Then at my phone.

Then at the door again.

*Should have gone with her.*

I sighed.

*Common courtesy, idiot. At least you could have walked your wife to work.*

I stood up.

Then immediately stopped.

*Wife.*

I said the word quietly.

It felt strange.

Then strangely comforting.

I looked around the apartment again.

Her things were everywhere.

A small perfume bottle near the mirror.

A couple of notebooks.

A mug with some ridiculous little design on it.

Her slippers near the sofa.

Little pieces of her life.

And somehow, I was supposed to fit into all of this now.

I didn't know whether I was nervous.

Maybe I was.

Maybe I was just excited.

Or maybe I was still drunk enough from last night that my brain had stopped functioning properly.

Because I still couldn't remember how I had proposed.

I remembered going to the company dinner.

I remembered Sylvia being there.

I remembered everyone drinking.

I remembered deliberately avoiding alcohol.

I always did.

I wasn't good with it.

Apparently, someone had mixed some into my juice anyway.

Very little.

But apparently, that was enough.

After that?

Nothing.

A blank space.

I knew only what people had told me.

Apparently, I had proposed.

Apparently, I had called my parents.

Apparently, I had introduced her.

Apparently, I had somehow managed to get us engaged.

And apparently...

I had been very serious about it.

I looked at the ring on my finger.

Then smiled faintly.

> "Guess I really meant it."

I work as a software engineer.

My package was more than enough to support myself, and eventually both of us if needed.

Sylvia worked as a consultant in a marketing firm and earned almost around the same.

So financially, neither of us needed to depend on the other.

That wasn't the strange part.

The strange part was that I had apparently decided to spend the rest of my life with someone.

Me.

The person who never told anyone what kind of music he liked.

The person who never discussed his ideal partner.

The person whose colleagues knew I could probably solve almost any technical problem they threw at me...

Yet couldn't tell you what my favourite food was.

I liked keeping those things private.

Work was work.

Personal life was personal.

People knew I was social.

They knew I could talk.

They knew I could joke.

They knew I could work with almost anyone.

But somehow nobody really knew me.

Not properly.

And now...

There was a girl who was apparently going to know all of it.

I glanced at the kitchen.

The food was still there.

I smiled again.

And she cooks.

I was already imagining ridiculous things.

Coming home from work.

Eating together.

Arguing over something stupid.

Cooking together.

Maybe travelling somewhere.

Maybe waking up beside her.

Maybe—

I stopped myself.

*Enough.*

I was already planning married life after one breakfast.

I picked up my bag.

Then looked toward the door.

I could have taken a cab.

Instead, I decided to walk.

Maybe I deserved the extra twenty minutes.

After all...

I hadn't even walked my future wife to work.

What kind of husband was I?

I stepped outside.

The city moved around me.

Cars.

People.

Traffic.

Shops.

Everything looked completely normal.

Yet I felt like I was walking through a dream.

A strangely sweet one.

I didn't know what had happened last night.

I didn't know why I had proposed.

I didn't know what Sylvia thought of me.

And most importantly...

I didn't know why seeing her smile had affected me so much.

But there was one thing I knew.

For the next fourteen days...

I was going to live with her.

And somehow, the thought didn't scare me.

It made my heart beat a little faster.

I smiled to myself.

I had no idea what was going to become of me.

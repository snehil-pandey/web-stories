# Chapter 11: The Photograph

Sunday morning was supposed to be peaceful.

It wasn't.

Sylvia was standing in front of the cupboard, looking for something.

> "Where did you keep the old albums?"

I looked up from my laptop.

> "Which albums?"

> "The photo albums."

> "I don't know."

She turned around.

> "You don't know where your own photos are?"

> "I don't look at them."

She stared at me.

> "Why?"

> "Because they're old."

> "That is literally what old photos are."

I went back to my screen.

She opened another cupboard.

Then another.

Finally, she found a small box.

> "Aha."

She brought it to the sofa.

I looked at it.

> "You found them."

> "Yes."

She sat down.

> "Come here."

> "I have work."

> "Five minutes."

I closed my laptop.

> "Fine."

She opened the box.

There were old photographs inside.

School.

College.

Family gatherings.

Friends.

Nothing particularly interesting.

Until she pulled out one photograph.

She looked at it.

Then at me.

> "This is you?"

> "Yes."

> "You look so different."

> "It was college."

She smiled.

> "You were cute."

> "I was normal."

> "No."

She looked at the photograph again.

> "You were cute."

I took it from her.

It was an old college photograph.

A group of us standing outside the campus building.

I barely remembered the day.

She leaned closer.

> "You're on the left."

> "Yes."

> "Who is beside you?"

> "A friend."

> "What were you doing that day?"

> "I don't remember."

She laughed.

> "You don't remember anything."

> "Not everything needs to be remembered."

She smiled.

> "Maybe."

Then she looked at another photograph.

This time, she didn't speak.

I noticed.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "You stopped talking."

> "I was looking."

> "At?"

She turned the photograph slightly.

It showed me standing with a few friends near a metro station.

I frowned.

> "Where was this?"

> "Don't you know?"

> "No."

She stared at the photograph.

> "You don't remember this?"

> "No."

Her expression changed.

Only slightly.

But I noticed.

> "Why?"

She shook her head.

> "Nothing."

I took the photograph.

> "Where is this?"

She hesitated.

> "The metro."

I looked at it again.

> "Which metro?"

She gave me the name.

I thought for a moment.

> "I used to take that line."

> "I know."

I looked at her.

> "You know?"

She immediately corrected herself.

> "I mean, I guessed."

> "You're bad at guessing."

She laughed nervously.

> "Maybe."

I studied her face.

Something was wrong.

Not bad.

Just hidden.

And Sylvia wasn't very good at hiding things from me anymore.

I placed the photograph down.

> "Have we met before?"

She froze.

For one second.

Then smiled.

> "What?"

> "Before the company deal."

She looked away.

> "No."

The answer came too quickly.

I noticed.

But I didn't push.

> "Okay."

She looked relieved.

I picked up another photograph.

This one was from college.

A group of people standing near a building.

Nothing unusual.

Then Sylvia suddenly pointed.

> "That's your friend, right?"

> "Yes."

> "The one who called you?"

I looked at her.

> "How do you remember that?"

She went completely still.

I stared at her.

She stared back.

Neither of us spoke.

Then she laughed.

> "I've heard you talk about him."

I hadn't.

I knew I hadn't.

But I didn't say that.

Instead, I asked,

> "Have I?"

> "Yes."

> "Recently?"

She hesitated.

> "Maybe."

I smiled faintly.

> "You're lying."

Her face became red.

> "I am not."

> "You are."

She closed the album.

> "Fine."

I looked at her.

She hugged the album against herself.

> "Can I tell you something?"

> "Yes."

She took a breath.

> "I knew your name before you knew mine."

That sentence made everything quiet.

> "What?"

She looked at me.

> "I knew your name."

> "When?"

She hesitated.

> "A long time ago."

> "How long?"

She looked down.

> "Years."

I didn't speak.

She continued.

> "Five years."

Five years.

My mind immediately began connecting pieces.

The photograph.

The metro.

Her questions.

Her knowledge about things I didn't remember telling her.

I looked at her.

> "Where did you see me?"

She didn't answer.

> "Sylvia."

She looked nervous now.

Something I had almost never seen.

> "I'll tell you."

> "When?"

She smiled faintly.

> "When I'm ready."

I leaned back.

> "Okay."

She looked surprised.

> "You're not going to ask?"

> "I already did."

> "And?"

> "You said you'll tell me."

She smiled.

> "You really trust me that much?"

> "Yes."

Her eyes softened.

Then she quietly said,

> "Thank you."

---

The rest of the morning passed normally.

At least on the surface.

I worked.

She cooked.

We talked about groceries.

Nothing unusual.

But my mind kept returning to those words.

Five years.

I didn't know her five years ago.

At least, I didn't remember her.

Maybe I had seen her.

Maybe we had crossed paths.

Maybe she had noticed me somewhere.

The possibilities were endless.

But one thing bothered me.

She had remembered.

I hadn't.

I looked toward the kitchen.

She was humming quietly while preparing lunch.

I smiled.

What exactly happened five years ago?

I wanted to know.

But strangely...

I didn't want to force her to tell me.

Whatever it was, she had carried it alone for a long time.

And if she was finally ready to share it with me...

I'd rather hear it from her.

---

That evening, we went for a walk.

No destination.

Just the two of us.

Sylvia was quieter than usual.

After a while, she said,

> "Are you angry?"

> "No."

> "Suspicious?"

> "Yes."

She laughed.

> "At least you're honest."

> "I don't know why you know so much about me."

She looked at me.

> "I've been paying attention."

> "For five years?"

She smiled.

> "Maybe."

I looked ahead.

> "That is a long time."

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

She didn't answer immediately.

Then she said,

> "Because some people are worth remembering."

I looked at her.

She was looking straight ahead.

Her face was calm.

But her ears were slightly red.

I smiled.

> "That's a dangerous thing to say."

> "Why?"

> "Because I might remember it."

She looked at me.

> "Then remember it."

And for some reason...

I couldn't.

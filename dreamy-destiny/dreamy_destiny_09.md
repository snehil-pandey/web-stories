# Chapter 9: One Umbrella

The rain started at exactly the wrong time.

I was standing outside the office building, looking at the sky.

Dark clouds.

Heavy rain.

And, of course, no umbrella.

I checked my phone.

One message.

**Sylvia:** *Where are you?*

I replied.

**Me:** *Outside.*

Her response came immediately.

**Sylvia:** *Wait.*

I didn't know what she meant.

A few minutes later, I saw her.

She came running out of the building with an umbrella in her hand.

Her hair had become slightly messy from the wind.

She stopped beside me.

> "You were actually going to walk home like this?"

> "I was considering it."

She stared at me.

> "You were considering walking in the rain?"

> "Yes."

> "Without an umbrella?"

> "Yes."

She sighed.

> "Come here."

She opened the umbrella.

I stepped under it.

There wasn't much space.

The umbrella was clearly designed for one person.

Not two.

Our shoulders touched.

I immediately became aware of it.

Too close.

I looked straight ahead.

Sylvia adjusted the umbrella.

> "Move closer."

> "I am close."

> "You're still outside."

I looked down.

Half my shoulder was getting wet.

I moved closer.

Our arms touched.

She became quiet.

I became quieter.

The rain grew louder.

For some reason, neither of us spoke.

---

We started walking.

The road was crowded.

Cars moved slowly through the rain.

People hurried past us.

The umbrella kept tilting because of the wind.

Sylvia held it firmly.

> "You're getting wet."

> "So are you."

> "I have the umbrella."

> "Your shoulder is outside."

She looked.

It was.

She moved the umbrella toward herself.

> "You're right."

I shifted it back.

> "Now you're getting wet."

She looked at me.

> "Then what do we do?"

I took the umbrella from her.

> "Give it to me."

She let go.

I held it above both of us.

She looked at me.

> "You're taller."

> "Yes."

> "That's unfair."

> "What is?"

> "You get the umbrella and I get the rain."

I tilted it slightly toward her.

> "Better?"

She smiled.

> "Much."

We continued.

After a while, she quietly said,

> "You know, you're different when we're alone."

I looked at her.

> "How?"

> "You talk more."

> "I barely talk."

> "Exactly."

I smiled.

She laughed.

> "See?"

> "What?"

> "That."

> "What?"

> "That little smile."

I looked away.

> "It's nothing."

> "It isn't."

She walked closer.

Our shoulders touched again.

This time, neither of us moved.

---

We reached a crossing.

The traffic signal was red.

We stopped.

Sylvia looked at the rain.

Then at me.

> "You like rain?"

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

> "It's quiet."

She smiled.

> "You always like quiet things."

> "Maybe."

> "Then why did you agree to marry someone who talks so much?"

I looked at her.

> "That was probably my mistake."

She gasped.

> "Muse!"

I laughed.

She lightly hit my arm.

> "You're horrible."

> "I was joking."

> "I know."

She smiled.

Then became quiet again.

The signal turned green.

We crossed.

Halfway through, a car passed too quickly through a puddle.

Water splashed toward us.

I instinctively moved Sylvia closer to me.

She froze.

The umbrella tilted.

For a moment, she was almost pressed against my side.

I looked down.

She looked up.

Her face was red.

> "You okay?"

She nodded.

> "Yes."

Neither of us moved immediately.

Then she quietly said,

> "Thank you."

I released her.

> "Careful."

She nodded.

We continued walking.

But now she was closer than before.

And neither of us seemed interested in fixing that.

---

A few minutes later, the rain became heavier.

The umbrella shook in the wind.

Sylvia reached for my arm.

> "Hold this properly."

> "I am."

> "No, your hand is slipping."

She placed her hand over mine.

Both of us stopped.

Her fingers rested against mine.

Warm.

Soft.

Completely still.

She realized what she had done.

Her eyes widened.

She immediately tried to pull away.

But the umbrella tilted.

I caught her hand before it slipped.

> "Wait."

She looked at me.

> "I've got it."

Her fingers remained in my hand.

For some reason, I didn't let go.

Neither did she.

We walked like that.

One umbrella.

One hand holding it.

The other somehow holding hers.

Neither of us mentioned it.

My heartbeat had become ridiculous.

Calm down.

It didn't listen.

---

We reached the apartment building.

The rain was still falling heavily.

Sylvia looked at our hands.

Then at me.

> "You can let go now."

I looked down.

Only then did I realize we were still holding hands.

I released her.

> "Sorry."

She shook her head.

> "Why are you apologizing?"

> "I don't know."

She smiled.

> "You really don't know, do you?"

> "What?"

She stepped closer.

Her voice became quieter.

> "You don't realize what you do sometimes."

I looked at her.

> "What did I do?"

She smiled.

> "Nothing."

> "That isn't an answer."

> "I know."

She turned toward the entrance.

Then stopped.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Did you enjoy today?"

I thought about it.

> "Yes."

> "Even the rain?"

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "Good."

She opened the door.

We entered.

---

Later that night, I was sitting at the dining table.

Sylvia was drying her hair.

She walked past me.

Then stopped.

> "Muse."

> "Yes?"

> "Your hand."

I looked at it.

There was a faint red mark from holding the umbrella too tightly.

She noticed.

> "You held it like that the whole way?"

> "Probably."

She shook her head.

> "You're ridiculous."

She brought a small tube of cream and placed it beside me.

> "Use this."

> "It's nothing."

> "I know."

She looked at me.

> "But use it anyway."

Then she walked away.

I looked at the cream.

Then toward her room.

Why does she care about such small things?

I didn't have an answer.

Maybe I didn't need one.

---

A little later, she came back.

She stood near the table.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "You know what I was going to say yesterday?"

I looked up.

She smiled.

> "No."

She seemed to hesitate.

Then she shook her head.

> "Forget it."

I stared at her.

She smiled.

> "Good night."

> "Good night."

She turned away.

I watched her disappear down the hallway.

Again.

She had almost said something.

Again.

And again, I let her go without asking.

Maybe because some things were better when they arrived naturally.

Or maybe because I was afraid of what her answer might do to me.

I leaned back.

Outside, the rain continued.

I could still remember her hand in mine.

The warmth.

The silence.

The way she had looked at me.

I closed my eyes.

This is getting dangerous.

Not because I was falling for her.

At least, that was what I told myself.

But because I had started wondering what it would feel like...

if she held my hand tomorrow too.

# Chapter 2: Two Weeks

The first thing I noticed after reaching work was that I was smiling.

Not visibly.

At least, I hoped not.

But there was something different about me.

I was sitting in front of my monitor, looking at a block of code that had been refusing to cooperate with me since yesterday.

Normally, this would have irritated me.

Today?

I was strangely calm.

I leaned back in my chair.

Two weeks.

I looked at the date again.

Fourteen days.

That was all.

Fourteen days until I was supposed to get married.

I looked at the code again.

A function was failing because of a stupid little condition.

I fixed it.

The build passed.

I stared at the screen.

> "That's it?"

The guy sitting beside me looked over.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

He looked suspiciously at me.

> "You look happy."

I immediately looked back at the screen.

> I don't.

> "You do."

> I fixed the bug.

> "That doesn't make you happy."

> "It does."

He laughed.

> "Whatever you say."

I ignored him.

My fingers continued moving across the keyboard.

One task finished.

Another notification appeared.

Then another.

I opened both.

My mind slipped naturally into work mode.

That was the easy part.

Work had rules.

Problems had causes.

Code either worked or it didn't.

People were harder.

Especially one particular person.

I glanced at my phone.

No messages.

I put it back down.

Five minutes later, I checked again.

Nothing.

I frowned.

Why am I checking?

I put the phone away again.

Another ten minutes passed.

I checked.

Still nothing.

*Man.*

I rubbed my forehead.

*This is embarrassing.*

I had known Sylvia for approximately one day.

And somehow I was already waiting for her messages.

Not that I would admit that.

To anyone.

Especially myself.

My phone finally vibrated.

I looked down.

**Sylvia:** *Did you reach safely?*

I stared at the message.

A smile appeared before I could stop it.

I typed.

**Me:** *Yes.*

I looked at it.

Too dry.

I added:

**Me:** *You?*

She replied almost immediately.

**Sylvia:** *Obviously. I am at work.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *Good.*

Three dots appeared.

Then disappeared.

Then appeared again.

**Sylvia:** *You are really bad at conversations.*

I stared at the screen.

*Excuse me?*

I typed:

**Me:** *I was not aware this was an interview.*

Her reply came quickly.

**Sylvia:** *It isn't.*

Then:

**Sylvia:** *But you are my fiancé.*

I stopped.

That word again.

*Fiancé.*

I read it twice.

Then once more.

My fingers hovered above the keyboard.

I finally typed:

**Me:** *Right.*

She sent a laughing emoji.

I stared at it.

Then put the phone down.

For some reason, my face felt warm.

I looked around the office.

Nobody seemed to have noticed.

Good.

I had a reputation to maintain.

A few minutes later, another message arrived.

**Sylvia:** *How much do you remember?*

My fingers stopped.

That question.

I looked at the screen for a long moment.

Then typed honestly.

**Me:** *Almost nothing.*

Her reply didn't come immediately this time.

**Sylvia:** *Nothing?*

**Me:** *I remember the dinner.*

**Me:** *I remember you were there.*

**Me:** *After that, things become unclear.*

There was another pause.

Then:

**Sylvia:** *Do you remember talking to me?*

I frowned.

I tried to search through the empty space in my memory.

Nothing.

I remembered her sitting somewhere across the room.

People talking.

Glasses on the table.

Music.

Then nothing.

**Me:** *No.*

The typing indicator appeared.

Stopped.

Appeared again.

Finally:

**Sylvia:** *That's unfortunate.*

I stared at those two words.

Something about them felt strange.

Not angry.

Not disappointed.

Almost amused.

**Me:** *Did I do something embarrassing?*

The answer came surprisingly fast.

**Sylvia:** *Very.*

I froze.

Very?

**Me:** *How embarrassing?*

**Sylvia:** *Extremely.*

I leaned back in my chair.

> "Great."

The guy beside me looked over.

> "What happened?"

> "Nothing."

> "You definitely said something."

> "Work."

He turned away.

I looked at my phone again.

**Me:** *Did I at least behave properly?*

This time Sylvia took longer.

**Sylvia:** *Define properly.*

I closed my eyes.

I'm doomed.

I typed:

**Me:** *Did I hurt anyone?*

**Sylvia:** *No.*

Good.

**Me:** *Did I insult anyone?*

**Sylvia:** *Not exactly.*

Not exactly?

That was worse.

**Me:** *Did I break anything?*

**Sylvia:** *No.*

I relaxed slightly.

Then another message arrived.

**Sylvia:** *You proposed.*

My hand stopped moving.

I stared at the screen.

There it was.

The thing I had been trying to understand since morning.

**Me:** *To you?*

I immediately regretted sending it.

Obviously it was to her.

She replied:

**Sylvia:** *No. To the waiter.*

I blinked.

Then laughed.

A quiet laugh escaped me before I could stop it.

**Me:** *Very funny.*

**Sylvia:** *I know.*

I stared at her message.

She really was confident.

Even through text.

**Me:** *So I proposed to you.*

**Sylvia:** *Yes.*

**Me:** *And you said yes.*

There was another pause.

**Sylvia:** *Yes.*

I leaned back.

For some reason, that simple word made my chest feel warm.

I looked at the ring on my finger.

Then back at the phone.

**Me:** *Why?*

This time there was a long silence.

Long enough that I wondered whether I had asked something I shouldn't have.

Then:

**Sylvia:** *Because I wanted to.*

I looked at that sentence for a while.

No explanation.

No hesitation.

Just that.

Because I wanted to.

I smiled faintly.

Fair enough.

I typed:

**Me:** *Then I suppose I have no complaints.*

Her response:

**Sylvia:** *Good.*

Then another:

**Sylvia:** *Because I am not giving you the option now.*

I laughed again.

This girl...

I put my phone down.

But the conversation stayed in my head.

Because I wanted to.

I didn't know why that answer affected me so much.

Maybe because I couldn't remember choosing her.

Yet she remembered.

She knew.

And somehow, that made me feel less afraid.

---

By evening, I returned to the apartment.

I opened the door.

> "I'm home."

No answer.

I took off my shoes.

The apartment was quiet.

I looked around.

Then heard something from the kitchen.

I walked over.

Sylvia was standing there with her sleeves rolled up, looking at something on the counter.

She turned.

> "Oh."

Her expression brightened.

> "You're back."

> "Yeah."

> "How was work?"

> "Fine."

She smiled.

> "That's it?"

> "What else?"

She shook her head.

> "You really are difficult."

I placed my bag down.

> "You already knew that."

> "Actually, I didn't."

She looked at me for a second.

> "Yesterday I thought you were just quiet."

> "And now?"

> "Now I think you are selectively quiet."

I raised an eyebrow.

> "That sounds suspiciously specific."

> "It is."

She went back to what she was doing.

I watched her for a moment.

> "What are you making?"

> "Dinner."

> "You're cooking again?"

She looked over her shoulder.

> "Yes."

> "You don't have to."

> "I know."

> "Then why?"

> "Because I want to."

I stopped.

The same words.

Again.

Because I want to.

I looked away.

> "Okay."

She smiled slightly.

> "Go wash your hands."

> "Yes, ma'am."

She laughed.

I walked toward the sink.

For some reason, the apartment already felt different from the morning.

It didn't feel like a place I had been sent to.

It felt like somewhere I had returned to.

That realization caught me off guard.

I looked at my reflection in the kitchen window.

Straight face.

Calm expression.

Nothing unusual.

Good.

Nobody could see the chaos underneath.

Not even her.

At least...

I hoped not.

After dinner, Sylvia sat across from me.

She rested her chin on her hand.

> "So."

I looked up.

> "So?"

> "You really don't remember anything?"

> "Not much."

She watched me carefully.

> "What do you remember about me?"

I thought about it.

> "You work in consulting."

She nodded.

> "You're good at your work."

Her eyebrows lifted slightly.

> "How do you know?"

> "I've seen you at company dinners."

> "Anything else?"

I paused.

> "You don't like being late."

> "True."

> "You prefer things organized."

> "Mostly."

> "You drink water when everyone else drinks alcohol."

She went silent.

I looked at her.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

She looked away.

I frowned.

> "Did I say something wrong?"

> "No."

She smiled.

> "Keep going."

I thought again.

> "You're confident."

Her smile became smaller.

> "You're straightforward."

She nodded.

> "You're good at talking to people."

> "That's my job."

> "And..."

I stopped.

She waited.

> "And you smile a lot."

She blinked.

For the first time since I had met her, Sylvia seemed to have run out of an immediate answer.

She looked down.

> "Oh."

I wasn't sure why.

> "You don't?"

> "I do."

She tucked a strand of hair behind her ear.

> "Just..."

She paused.

> "Not always."

I looked at her.

Something about that answer felt oddly personal.

Before I could ask, she stood up.

> "I'll make coffee."

> "I can do it."

She looked back.

> "You know how?"

> "Yes."

> "Do you?"

> "Yes."

> "Then prove it."

I stood up.

> "Fine."

She moved aside.

I started making coffee.

She watched.

> "You're measuring it."

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

> "Consistency."

She stared at me.

> "Of course."

I looked at her.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

She smiled.

I handed her the cup.

She took it.

Our fingers touched for barely a second.

Both of us stopped.

It was nothing.

Just a touch.

Yet somehow the room became quieter.

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

Neither said anything.

Then she quietly took the cup.

> "Thank you."

I nodded.

> "No problem."

She walked away.

I stayed there for another second.

What was that?

I looked down at my hand.

Nothing.

Absolutely nothing.

And yet my heart had decided otherwise.

I went back to the sofa.

Sylvia sat beside me this time.

Not across.

Beside.

There was a little distance between us.

Still...

Closer than before.

She looked at her coffee.

Then at me.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Are you nervous about getting married?"

I thought about lying.

I didn't.

> "A little."

She smiled.

> "Me too."

I looked at her.

> "You don't seem nervous."

> "I'm good at hiding it."

I almost laughed.

> "Apparently, I'm not the only one."

She looked at me.

Then smiled.

> "Apparently."

For some reason, neither of us moved away.

Outside, the city continued making noise.

Inside, everything was quiet.

Fourteen days.

I looked at her.

Then at the ring.

Then back at her.

Maybe I didn't remember how this started.

Maybe I didn't know what I had said that night.

Maybe I had somehow walked into a marriage without remembering taking the first step.

But sitting there beside her...

I had a strange feeling.

Maybe remembering everything wasn't as important as I thought.

Maybe I could make new memories.

Starting now.

I looked away before she could notice the smile on my face.

She noticed anyway.

She said nothing.

She simply smiled into her coffee.

And somehow...

That was enough.

# Chapter 6: A Very Ordinary Date

I had never understood why people called certain evenings memorable.

Usually, nothing special happened.

No grand confession.

No dramatic event.

No perfectly planned moment.

Just an ordinary evening that somehow stayed in your head longer than it had any right to.

That was exactly what happened that day.

It started with a message.

**Sylvia:** *Are you free today?*

I looked at it while walking out of the office.

**Me:** *Yes.*

Three dots appeared almost immediately.

**Sylvia:** *That's it?*

I stopped walking.

> "What else was I supposed to say?"

I typed:

**Me:** *Yes, I'm free.*

She replied:

**Sylvia:** *You are very expressive.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *Thank you.*

Her reply came after a few seconds.

**Sylvia:** *That was sarcasm.*

I put my phone away.

I knew that.

Probably.

A few seconds later, I checked the phone again.

**Sylvia:** *Come to the café near my office.*

I stared at the message.

Then typed:

**Me:** *Why?*

**Sylvia:** *Because I said so.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *Okay.*

---

The café was only a short distance from her office.

When I arrived, Sylvia was already there.

She was sitting near the window, still wearing her work clothes, her laptop bag beside her.

She noticed me.

Her expression immediately softened.

> "You came."

> "You asked me to."

> "I wasn't sure you would."

> "Why?"

She shrugged.

> "You look like someone who would say yes and then forget."

> "I don't forget."

She smiled.

> "I know."

I sat down.

She pushed a menu toward me.

> "Choose something."

I looked at it.

> "Coffee."
> "Obviously."

> "What?"

> "You always choose coffee."

> "Because I like coffee."

> "I know."

I looked at her.

She smiled.

> "See? I'm learning."

I looked back at the menu.

She's enjoying this.

I ordered coffee.

She ordered something cold.

Then she leaned back.

> "So."

> "So?"

> "What did you do today?"

> "Work."

She stared at me.

> "That is not an answer."

> "It was work."

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Tell me something that isn't about work."

I thought.

Nothing immediately came to mind.

That was embarrassing.

> "I had lunch."

She stared.

Then burst out laughing.

> "You are unbelievable."

> "What?"

> "I asked about your day."

> "That was part of my day."

> "You really don't know how to talk about yourself, do you?"

I looked down.

> "No."

Her smile faded slightly.

Not because she was upset.

Because she was thinking.

Then she said,

> "Okay."

She leaned forward.

> "I'll ask specific questions."

I nodded.

> "Favorite movie?"

> "I don't have one."

> "Favorite song?"

> "Depends."

> "Favorite place?"

> "Home."

> "You already said that."

> "Then why ask again?"

She laughed.

> "Fine. Favorite food?"

I thought.

> "Anything mild."

> "I knew that."

> "Then why ask?"

> "To see if you'd change your answer."

I looked at her.

> "You're testing me?"

> "Maybe."

> "Why?"

She smiled.

> "I want to know you."

Something in my chest moved.

I looked away.

> "Okay."

She waited.

I looked at her.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "You were about to say something."

> "No."

> "You were."

> "I wasn't."

She smiled.

> "You're doing it again."

> "What?"

> "Hiding."

I didn't answer.

The coffee arrived.

Perfect timing.

I took a sip.

She watched me.

Then smiled.

> "You really are difficult."

> "Sorry."

> "I didn't say it was bad."

That made me look at her.

She was already looking away.

---

We stayed there much longer than planned.

One coffee became another.

Then we started talking about work.

She told me about a client who had spent three weeks changing their requirements every other day.

I listened.

She became animated while explaining it.

Her hands moved when she talked.

Her voice became faster.

Her expressions changed constantly.

She wasn't the composed consultant I had seen from a distance.

She was just Sylvia.

And somehow, that version was much more interesting.

> "So then I told him if you change the entire strategy every time someone gives you an opinion, you don't need a consultant. You need a coin."

I laughed.

She stopped.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "You laughed."

> "Yes."

> "At me?"

> "No."

> "Then?"

> "You were funny."

She smiled.

> "Good."

Then she continued.

I listened.

And for once, I didn't think about work.

---

When we finally left, it was already dark.

Sylvia checked her phone.

> "It's late."

> "Yes."

> "You hungry?"

> "A little."

> "There is a place nearby."

We walked.

Neither of us said much.

The city was still busy around us.

Cars passed.

People moved along the streets.

Shop signs reflected against the pavement.

Yet somehow, walking beside her made everything feel quieter.

We reached the restaurant.

She stopped outside.

> "Have you been here?"

> "No."

> "Me neither."

> "Then why did you bring me here?"

She smiled.

> "Because it looked nice."

I looked through the glass.

It did.

Warm lights.

Small tables.

Nothing fancy.

Just comfortable.

> "Okay."

She smiled.

> "Okay."

---

Dinner was surprisingly good.

We talked about everything except marriage.

Our childhoods.

College.

Embarrassing moments.

Work.

Friends.

Things we liked.

Things we disliked.

At some point, Sylvia asked,

> "What did you want to become when you were a kid?"

> "Scientist."

She smiled.

> "Really?"

> "Yes."

> "What happened?"

> "I discovered computers."

> "That explains everything."

> "What does that mean?"

> "You found something you could control."

I looked at her.

She laughed.

> "I'm joking."

> "Are you?"

She paused.

> "Maybe not."

I smiled.

She noticed.

> "You smiled."

> "I do that sometimes."

> "Rarely."

> "Still counts."

She smiled.

> "Yes."

For some reason, neither of us looked away immediately.

Then she lowered her eyes.

> "Eat."

I picked up my fork.

> "Okay."

---

After dinner, we started walking back.

Halfway there, Sylvia suddenly stopped.

> "Wait."

I stopped too.

She pointed toward a small street.

> "There."

> "What?"

> "There's an ice cream place."

I looked.

> "You want ice cream?"

> "Yes."

> "It is cold."

> "I know."

> "Then why?"

> "Because I want ice cream."

I nodded.

> "Okay."

She smiled.

> "You agree too easily."

> "You asked."

> "That isn't the point."

> "What is the point?"

She looked at me.

> "I don't know."

I smiled.

She laughed.

We went inside.

She ordered chocolate.

I ordered nothing.

She looked at me.

> "You're not getting one?"

> "No."

> "You don't like ice cream?"

> "I do."

> "Then?"

> "I'm not hungry."

She narrowed her eyes.

> "You said you were hungry ten minutes ago."

> "I was."

> "And now?"

> "I'm not."

She stared.

Then took her ice cream.

We walked outside.

She took one bite.

Then held it toward me.

> "Try."

I looked at it.

> "No."

> "Why?"

> "I don't need it."

> "Just one bite."

> "I said no."

She sighed.

> "Fine."

She took another bite.

A few seconds later, I looked at her.

She noticed.

> "You want some."

> "No."

> "You're looking at it."

> "I am looking at you."

The words escaped.

Silence.

She stopped walking.

I stopped too.

Her face slowly turned red.

I realized what I'd said.

*Great.*

I looked away.

> "I mean..."

She smiled.

> "You mean?"

I couldn't think of anything.

She laughed softly.

> "It's okay."

Then she held the ice cream toward me again.

> "One bite."

I took it.

She smiled.

> "See?"

I nodded.

> "Good."

We kept walking.

I could still feel the cold sweetness on my tongue.

But strangely, I remembered the way she had smiled more clearly.

---

When we reached the apartment, Sylvia unlocked the door.

She entered first.

Then turned around.

> "Thank you."

> "For what?"

> "Today."

I shrugged.

> "It was nothing."

She looked at me.

> "Don't say that."

> "Why?"

> "Because it wasn't nothing."

I became quiet.

She put her bag down.

> "I had a good time."

I looked at her.

> "So did I."

She smiled.

> "Really?"

> "Yes."

> "You don't look like it."

I frowned.

> "How am I supposed to look?"

> "Happy."

I thought about it.

> "I'm happy."

She smiled.

> "Good."

Then she walked toward her room.

> "Muse?"

> "Hmm?"

> "Tomorrow, I'm going to ask you more questions."

I smiled.

> "Why?"

> "Because I'm still trying to figure you out."

She disappeared down the hallway.

I stood there.

*Figure me out.*

For some reason, the idea didn't bother me.

Actually...

I think I liked it.

I sat on the sofa.

The apartment was quiet again.

But it didn't feel empty anymore.

There was a strange warmth left behind from the evening.

Nothing had happened.

No important conversation.

No confession.

No dramatic moment.

Just coffee.

Dinner.

A walk.

Ice cream.

And her laughing beside me.

An ordinary evening.

Yet when I closed my eyes for a moment, I could still hear her voice.

And somehow...

I knew I would remember today.

Maybe some days didn't become special because something extraordinary happened.

Maybe they became special because of who you spent them with.

I leaned back against the sofa.

Then quietly smiled.

Two weeks.

The thought didn't scare me anymore.

It felt surprisingly short

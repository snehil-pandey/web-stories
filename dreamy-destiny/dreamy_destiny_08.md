# Chapter 8: The Almost Confession

The apartment was unusually quiet that night.

Sylvia had gone to her room after dinner.

I was sitting on the sofa with my laptop open.

There were three windows on the screen.

One contained the code I had been working on.

Another had documentation.

The third had a message from my team.

Normally, this was enough to occupy my mind completely.

Tonight, it wasn't.

I kept looking toward the hallway.

What is wrong with me?

I closed the laptop.

Maybe I was tired.

That was probably it.

I stood up and went to the kitchen for water.

When I came back, Sylvia was standing there.

She had changed into comfortable clothes and was holding two cups.

> "You're still awake?"

> "So are you."

> "I was going to ask you something."

She placed one cup on the table.

I looked at it.

> "Tea?"

> "Yes."

> "I don't drink tea."

> "I know."

She smiled.

> "It's mine."

> "Then why did you bring two cups?"

> "So you could sit with me."

I looked at the sofa.

Then at her.

> "Okay."

We sat down.

For a while, neither of us said anything.

She took a sip.

I drank water.

She glanced at me.

> "You know what I've noticed?"

> "That you talk a lot?"

She immediately looked offended.

> "I do not."

> "You do."

> "I talk appropriately."

> "That's not what your colleagues said."

She narrowed her eyes.

> "You remember that?"

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "So you do listen."

> "I told you."

> "You pretend not to."

I looked at her.

She was smiling.

Then her expression slowly became quieter.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Why did you ask?"

> "Ask what?"

> "To marry me."

I became still.

The question was simple.

The answer wasn't.

I looked down at the glass in my hand.

> "I don't remember."

> "I know."

There was no disappointment in her voice.

Just softness.

> "That's not what I mean."

I looked at her.

She placed the cup down.

> "When you woke up that first morning, you could have panicked."

> "I did."

> "You didn't show it."

> "I was panicking internally."

She laughed softly.

> "I know."

> "How?"

> "You kept looking at the door every few minutes."

I looked away.

She noticed that?

She continued.

> "You could have told your parents you wanted to cancel everything."

I didn't answer.

> "You could have asked me to leave."

I looked at her.

> "I wouldn't do that."

> "Why?"

I thought carefully.

> "I don't know."

She smiled faintly.

> "That answer again."

I leaned back.

> "I don't know what happened that night. But I know what happened afterward."

> "What happened afterward?"

> "You made breakfast."

She blinked.

I continued.

> "You didn't pressure me."

She listened quietly.

> "You explained things."

I looked at her.

> "And you've been patient."

Her eyes softened.

> "So?"

I took a breath.

> "So I don't feel like I made a mistake."

She looked down.

A small smile appeared on her face.

> "That's a very Muse answer."

> "What does that mean?"

> "It means you said something sweet without realizing you said something sweet."

I looked away.

*Was that sweet?*

Apparently it was.

---

She was quiet for a while.

Then she asked,

> "Do you trust me?"

> "Yes."

The answer came immediately.

She looked surprised.

> "That fast?"

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

> "Because you've never given me a reason not to."

She stared at me.

I suddenly realized how direct that sounded.

She lowered her eyes.

> "Thank you."

Silence returned.

But this time it felt different.

Not awkward.

Just full.

As if there were things sitting between us that neither of us knew how to move.

Sylvia slowly spoke.

> "Can I tell you something?"

> "Of course."

She looked at me.

> "I was scared."

That caught my attention.

> "Of me?"

She shook her head.

> "Of this."

She gestured vaguely between us.

> "Marriage?"

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

She thought for a moment.

> "Because I knew you before you knew me."

I frowned.

> "What does that mean?"

She immediately smiled.

> "Nothing."

> "You just said something."

> "I know."

> "So?"

> "Forget it."

I stared at her.

She looked away.

There was something there.

Something she wasn't telling me.

I could feel it.

But I didn't ask again.

Not because I wasn't curious.

Because I didn't want her to feel cornered.

After a few seconds, she quietly said,

> "I thought you might say no."

> "To marriage?"

> "Yes."

I smiled faintly.

> "Why would I?"

She looked at me.

> "Because you barely know me."

> "That is true."

She frowned.

> "That's not reassuring."

I laughed.

> "I'm saying I want to know you."

She became quiet.

Then asked,

> "How much?"

I looked at her.

> "As much as you'll let me."

Her eyes widened slightly.

I didn't understand why.

Then she smiled.

> "That's dangerous."

> "Why?"

> "Because I might let you."

I didn't know what to say.

She looked down at her hands.

I could see her fingers moving nervously.

That was unusual.

Sylvia wasn't nervous easily.

Not in meetings.

Not around strangers.

Not even around me most of the time.

But now...

She was.

I looked at her.

> "Are you okay?"

> "Yes."

> "You're nervous."

> "No."

> "You are."

She laughed softly.

> "Maybe."

> "Why?"

She didn't answer.

Instead, she asked,

> "What do you think of me?"

I stared at her.

That was an unfair question.

There were too many answers.

Smart.

Confident.

Kind.

Beautiful.

Annoyingly observant.

Too good at making me uncomfortable.

I settled on the safest one.

> "You're good."

She stared.

> "Again?"

> "What?"

> "That's all?"

I smiled.

> "You're amazing."

Her face immediately changed.

She looked away.

> "Better."

I watched her for a moment.

Then something slipped out before I could stop it.

> "You're also very beautiful."

Silence.

Complete silence.

Sylvia froze.

I froze.

Why did I say that?

She slowly turned toward me.

> "What?"

I looked at her.

> "You heard me."

> "I want you to say it again."

> "No."

> "Why?"

> "Because I already said it."

She smiled.

> "You're shy."

> "No."

> "You are."

> "I am not."

She laughed.

> "You're blushing."

I touched my cheek.

It felt warm.

*Damn.*

She laughed harder.

> "Okay, okay. I won't tease you."

I looked away.

Then heard her whisper,

> "Thank you."

This time, I didn't answer.

I didn't trust my mouth anymore.

---

A few minutes passed.

She leaned against the sofa.

Her voice became quieter.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "If I told you that I've liked you for a long time..."

I stopped breathing for half a second.

She continued.

> "Would that scare you?"

My mind went blank.

There were probably a hundred possible answers.

I could joke.

Change the subject.

Ask what she meant.

Pretend I didn't understand.

Instead, I said,

> "No."

She looked at me.

> "Really?"

> "No."

She smiled.

> "Good."

I looked at her.

She seemed relieved.

Then she opened her mouth.

> "I actually—"

Her phone rang.

We both looked at it.

She sighed.

> "Sorry."

She picked it up.

> "Hello?"

Her expression changed immediately.

Work.

Of course.

She listened.

Then began discussing something about a client presentation.

I sat there silently.

A strange feeling settled in my chest.

Not disappointment exactly.

Something close.

She eventually ended the call.

> "Sorry."

> "It's okay."

> "What were we talking about?"

I looked at her.

> "I don't remember."

She smiled.

> "You're terrible."

> "Probably."

She stood up.

> "I'll sleep."

> "Okay."

She took two steps.

Then stopped.

> "Muse?"

> "Hmm?"

She looked back.

> "Good night."

> "Good night."

She smiled.

Then walked away.

I stayed on the sofa.

My laptop was still sitting on the table.

I didn't open it.

I just sat there.

Thinking about what she had almost said.

I've liked you for a long time.

What did that mean?

How long?

Why me?

And why did the thought make my heart beat faster?

I leaned my head back.

Maybe I should have asked.

But something told me that when Sylvia was ready...

She would tell me herself.

And strangely, I was willing to wait.

Because for the first time in a long time...

I wasn't afraid of not knowing.

I was looking forward to finding out.

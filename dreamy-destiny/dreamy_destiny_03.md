# Chapter 3: The Other Side of the Table

The next morning, I woke up before my alarm.

That itself was unusual.

I usually needed the alarm.

Today, however, my eyes opened on their own.

I stared at the ceiling for a few seconds.

Then my mind caught up.

Apartment.

Wedding.

Fourteen days.

Sylvia.

I turned my head.

Her room was quiet.

For some reason, I smiled.

*This is becoming a habit.*

I got ready for work.

When I came out, I found a small note on the dining table.

**"Breakfast is in the kitchen. Don't skip it." — Sylvia.**

I stared at it.

Then at the kitchen.

Then back at the note.

I picked it up.

There was something oddly comforting about seeing her handwriting.

I folded the note once and slipped it into my wallet.

I immediately questioned myself.

*Why am I keeping this?*

I looked at it again.

*Whatever.*

I left for work.

---

By the time I reached the office, I was already back in my usual mode.

Headphones.

Laptop.

Code.

Tasks.

No unnecessary thoughts.

That was how things were supposed to be.

I had a meeting in twenty minutes.

Two pending bugs.

One client issue.

And a deployment that needed to be monitored.

Perfect.

I sat down.

My teammate looked at me.

> "You are early."

> "I know."

> "Something happened?"

> "No."

He looked at me for another second.

> "You're smiling again."

I stopped typing.

> "I am not."

> "You definitely are."

> "I am working."

> "You can work and smile."

> "Not relevant."

He laughed.

> "Fine, Mr. Serious."

I ignored him.

A notification appeared.

I opened the task.

And just like that, everything else disappeared.

For the next few hours, I barely looked away from my screen.

A problem appeared in one service.

I traced it.

Another appeared in a different module.

I switched.

Someone asked me a question while I was reviewing logs.

I answered without looking away.

Then a meeting started.

I joined.

Another engineer shared his screen.

I listened.

Someone suggested a solution.

I pointed out the problem.

Someone else suggested another.

I explained why that would create a different issue.

The meeting continued.

My phone vibrated once on the table.

I glanced at it.

A message from Sylvia.

**Sylvia:** *Did you eat?*

I looked at the time.

12:47.

I had not.

I typed:

**Me:** *Not yet.*

Her reply came almost immediately.

**Sylvia:** *Of course.*

Then:

**Sylvia:** *You are impossible.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *Meeting.*

**Sylvia:** *That is not an excuse.*

I put the phone face down.

My colleague noticed.

> "Girlfriend?"

I looked at him.

> "Fiancée."

He froze.

> "Wait."

Another colleague turned around.

> "What?"

He pointed at me.

> "This guy is engaged?"

I sighed.

Apparently, this was going to become a problem.

> "Yeah."

> "When?"

> "Recently."

> "And you didn't tell us?"

> "I forgot."

> "You forgot you're engaged?"

> "No."

I paused.

> "I forgot to tell you."

Everyone laughed.

One of them came over.

> "Show us."

> "No."

> "Come on."

> "No."

> "Why?"

I looked back at my screen.

> "Personal."

He stared at me.

> "You are seriously keeping your fiancée secret?"

> "I am not keeping her secret."

> "You haven't shown us a picture."

> "Because I don't want to."

He laughed.

> "Man, you're impossible."

Maybe.

I didn't particularly care.

There were certain things I didn't like sharing.

People at work knew enough about me.

They knew my skills.

My work.

My habits.

They knew I could handle difficult projects.

They knew I could stay calm during a production failure.

They knew I could sit for hours debugging something without getting irritated.

But they didn't know much beyond that.

That was intentional.

My personal life had always belonged to me.

And now...

I had Sylvia.

For some reason, the thought of everyone knowing about her made me uncomfortable.

Not because I was ashamed.

The opposite.

I didn't want something that suddenly felt precious to become another topic for everyone to discuss.

I looked at my phone.

Another message.

**Sylvia:** *Eat.*

I typed:

**Me:** *Yes, boss.*

Her reply:

**Sylvia:** *Good.*

I smiled again.

My colleague noticed.

> "Definitely girlfriend."

> "Fiancée."

> "Same thing."

> "No."

He laughed.

I went back to work.

---

Around three in the afternoon, I finally had some breathing room.

I opened my lunch.

Then noticed there was an extra container.

I stared at it.

I hadn't packed this.

I opened it.

There was a small portion of dessert.

I checked my phone.

No message.

I smiled.

She put this in.

I took a bite.

Then another.

It was good.

Very good.

I stopped.

She remembered I like this?

Then I realized something.

I had never told her.

I looked at the container again.

Maybe she had simply guessed.

Or maybe—

I stopped myself.

Don't overthink it.

I ate quietly.

Still smiling.

---

Across the city, Sylvia was having a very different day.

Her meeting room was full.

Three people were arguing over numbers.

One client representative wanted a revised proposal.

Her manager wanted the original strategy retained.

Another consultant was trying to reconcile both sides.

Sylvia listened.

Quietly.

Then placed her pen on the table.

> "Okay."

Everyone stopped.

She looked at the presentation.

> "If we keep the current strategy, we can meet the client's timeline."

She changed the slide.

> "But we won't meet the expected conversion target."

She changed another.

> "If we change the strategy completely, we improve the target but miss the deadline."

The client representative nodded.

> "So what do you suggest?"

Sylvia smiled.

> "We don't choose."

Someone frowned.

She continued.

> "We keep the current strategy for the first phase and change the second phase."

Her manager leaned forward.

> "That means additional work."

> "Yes."

> "And additional cost."

> "Yes."

The client looked uncertain.

Sylvia calmly explained the numbers.

Five minutes later, the room had gone from arguing to discussing implementation.

Her colleague beside her whispered,

> "How do you always do that?"

Sylvia didn't look at him.

> "Do what?"

> "Make everyone stop arguing."

> I don't.

> "You literally did."

She smiled.

> "I just gave them something else to argue about."

He laughed.

Her phone vibrated.

She looked down.

A message from Muse.

**Muse:** *The dessert was good.*

She stared at it.

Then smiled.

Her colleague noticed.

> "There it is."

She looked up.

> "What?"

> "That smile."

She immediately put her phone down.

> "Focus on the meeting."

> "You're the one smiling."

> "I am always professional."

> "Sure."

She ignored him.

But her fingers quietly touched the phone again.

She opened the message.

Then typed:

**Sylvia:** *You liked it?*

His response came after a minute.

**Muse:** *Yeah.*

She smiled.

**Sylvia:** *Good.*

She stared at the screen for a moment.

Then typed:

**Sylvia:** *I remembered.*

The reply didn't come immediately.

She wondered whether she had said too much.

Then:

**Muse:** *You remembered?*

She smiled.

**Sylvia:** *You mentioned it once.*

That wasn't true.

Not exactly.

He hadn't mentioned it.

She had noticed it.

But there was no reason to tell him that yet.

His reply arrived.

**Muse:** *I don't remember telling you.*

Sylvia's fingers stopped.

She stared at the screen.

Then smiled softly.

**Sylvia:** *You probably forgot.*

That was safer.

She put the phone away.

But the memory stayed with her.

She had watched him more carefully than he realized.

Long before this apartment.

Long before the ring.

Long before two weeks.

She shook the thought away.

Not now.

The meeting was still going.

---

By evening, Muse's office had become quieter.

Most people had left.

He was still working.

A colleague passed behind him.

> "You're still here?"

> "Almost done."

> "You know you can leave, right?"

> "I know."

> "Wedding preparations?"

Muse stopped typing.

> "Not today."

> "Your wedding is in two weeks, right?"

> "Yeah."

> "And you're still working this much?"

> "I have work."

The colleague laughed.

> "Man, you're going to be married. Relax."

Muse looked at the screen.

> "I am relaxed."

> "You don't look relaxed."

> "I never look relaxed."

> "That is true."

He walked away.

Muse finished the final task.

Saved everything.

Closed his laptop.

For a moment, he simply sat there.

Then he looked at his phone.

No new message.

He hesitated.

Then typed.

**Me:** *Are you still working?*

He stared at the message.

Why did I send that?

He almost deleted it.

Too late.

Seen.

Then a reply.

**Sylvia:** *Yes.*

**Me:** *When will you leave?*

**Sylvia:** *Probably another hour.*

I frowned.

Another hour?

I typed:

**Me:** *I'll wait.*

Three dots.

Then:

**Sylvia:** *You don't have to.*

**Me:** *I know.*

A pause.

**Sylvia:** *Then why?*

I stared at the screen.

I didn't have a good answer.

So I gave her the simplest one.

**Me:** *Because you're still working.*

Her reply took longer.

**Sylvia:** *Okay.*

Then:

**Sylvia:** *Wait for me.*

I smiled.

I put my phone away.

And for the first time that day...

I didn't mind waiting.

---

When Sylvia finally walked out of her office, she saw him immediately.

He was standing near the entrance.

Hands in his pockets.

Expression calm.

As if he had simply happened to be there.

She walked toward him.

> "You actually waited."

> "You told me to."

She smiled.

> "I didn't think you would."

> "Why?"

> "Because you look like the kind of person who hates waiting."

> "I do."

She raised an eyebrow.

> "Then?"

I looked at her.

> "You're different."

She stopped for half a second.

Her heart gave a small, traitorous beat.

She recovered quickly.

> "Good answer."

> "It wasn't an answer."

> "It sounded like one."

We started walking.

The evening air was cooler now.

Neither of us spoke for a while.

Then Sylvia asked,

> "How was your day?"

> "Busy."

> "That's vague."

> "Very busy."

She laughed.

> "That's even worse."

I looked at her.

> "How was yours?"

> "Long."

> "Successful?"

She glanced at me.

> "Yes."

> "I knew it."

> "You knew?"

> "You don't look tired."

She smiled.

> "You notice that?"

I looked ahead.

> "I notice things."  
> "Apparently."

We kept walking.

Then she said,

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "You're very different at work, aren't you?"

I glanced at her.

> "What do you mean?"

> "I saw you today."

I stopped walking.

She stopped too.

> "You saw me?"

> "Your office was nearby. I had a client meeting."

> "Oh."

She smiled.

> "You were surrounded by people."

> "I usually am."

> "You looked completely different."

I waited.

> "How?"

> "Confident."

I raised an eyebrow.

> "I'm confident at home too."

> "No."

She smiled.

> "At home, you're pretending not to be nervous."

I went silent.

She had noticed.

Of course she had.

She walked a little closer.

> "At work, you don't hide anything."

I looked at her.

> "That sounds wrong."

> "No."

She shook her head.

> "You hide plenty."

I stared at her.

She smiled.

> "But your work isn't one of them."

For some reason, I didn't know what to say.

She continued walking.

I followed.

After a few steps, she looked back.

> "Coming?"

> "Yeah."

I walked beside her.

She smiled.

And I realized something.

She was starting to understand me.

Maybe not completely.

But enough to notice the difference.

That should have made me uncomfortable.

Instead...

I found myself strangely relieved.

Maybe I didn't mind being understood by her.

Not at all.

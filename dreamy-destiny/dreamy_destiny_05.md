# Chapter 5: Things She Notices

There are things people notice about you.

And then there are things they notice without even realizing they are noticing them.

I had always assumed Sylvia belonged to the first category.

I was beginning to suspect I had been wrong.

It started with breakfast.

Again.

I walked into the kitchen and found her standing near the counter, checking something on her phone.

She looked up.

> "Good morning."

> "Morning."

> "You slept well?"

> "Yes."

> "Really?"

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "You look tired."

> "I am not."

> "You rubbed your eyes three times while walking here."

I stopped.

> "I did?"

> "Yes."

> "How did you notice that?"

She shrugged.

> "I notice things."

I looked at her.

There was something almost suspiciously familiar about that sentence.

> "You say that a lot."

> "Maybe because you keep giving me things to notice."

I didn't know how to respond to that.

So I sat down.

She placed breakfast in front of me.

I looked at it.

> "Again?"

> "Yes."

> "You didn't have to."

> "I know."

I looked at her.

She smiled.

> "Eat."

I started eating.

After a few bites, she sat across from me.

> "You don't like very sweet things."

My spoon stopped.

> "What?"

> "You don't."

> "How do you know?"

> "You didn't finish the sweet part of yesterday's dessert."

> "I was full."

> "You left the sweet part."

I looked at her.

She continued eating as if she hadn't just exposed herself.

> "You don't like too much sugar."

I frowned.

> "Maybe."

> "And you don't like tea."

I stared.

> "How do you know that?"

> "You refused it at the office dinner."

> "I don't remember doing that."

> "I do."

I became quiet.

She looked at me.

> "You drink coffee."

> "Yes."

> "Without much sugar."

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "See?"

> "See what?"

> "I notice things."

I looked down at my plate.

That was becoming mildly terrifying.

Not in a bad way.

Just...

She was paying attention.

A lot of attention.

I had spent years making sure people didn't know too much about me.

And now this woman was quietly collecting details I hadn't even realized I was giving away.

I took another bite.

I suddenly asked,

> "Do you like spicy food?"

She looked surprised.

> "Why?"

> "Just asking."

She smiled.

> "Yes. I like spicy food."

> "How spicy?"

> "Very."

> "And sweet things?"

> "Not too sweet."

She looked at me curiously.

> "Why are you asking?"

> "No reason."

That answer felt suspicious to her.

She narrowed her eyes.

I almost smiled.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "You're doing that thing again."

> "What thing?"

> "Looking at me like you're trying to solve a problem."

> "I am trying to understand you."

She paused.

Then smiled.

> "Good luck."

---

On the way to work, I kept thinking about it.

It was strange.

I knew a lot about Sylvia.

At least, I thought I did.

Her job.

Her company.

Her confidence.

Her professional reputation.

Her ability to handle difficult people.

Her habit of drinking water when everyone else drank alcohol.

But the more time I spent with her, the more I realized those were only surface details.

I didn't know what made her happy.

I didn't know what she hated.

I didn't know what kind of movies she liked.

I didn't know what she did when she was stressed.

I didn't know what she thought about when she couldn't sleep.

And somehow...

I wanted to know.

That thought stayed with me longer than it should have.

---

My day at work was busy.

There was a production issue.

Then a meeting.

Then another.

Then a teammate sent me a message asking whether I could review something.

I did.

Then another person called me.

I handled that too.

At some point, I realized I had been working for nearly four hours without checking my phone.

I looked at it.

There was a message from Sylvia.

**Sylvia:** *Lunch.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *I know.*

**Sylvia:** *Do you?*

**Me:** *Yes.*

**Sylvia:** *Then why haven't you eaten?*

I looked at the clock.

12:58.

I hadn't.

I typed:

**Me:** *Busy.*

She replied immediately.

**Sylvia:** *You are impossible.*

I smiled.

**Me:** *You said that already.*

**Sylvia:** *Because you keep proving me right.*

I put the phone down.

Then picked it up again.

**Me:** *Did you eat?*

Three dots.

**Sylvia:** *Yes.*

Then:

**Sylvia:** *See? I can take care of myself.*

I stared at the message.

I typed:

**Me:** *I know.*

She replied:

**Sylvia:** *Good.*

I put the phone away.

Then finally went to lunch.

My colleague looked at me.

> "You're smiling again."

> "I'm not."

> "You are."

I ignored him.

He looked at my phone.

> "Her?"

I didn't answer.

He laughed.

> "Definitely her."

---

Meanwhile, Sylvia had begun noticing something else.

Muse was predictable.

Not in his decisions.

In his little habits.

He always put his phone on the same side of the table.

He always kept his laptop charger folded in exactly the same way.

He never left a glass half full.

He closed doors quietly.

He checked the lock twice before sleeping.

He never interrupted when someone was speaking.

But when he was genuinely interested in something...

He forgot to pretend he wasn't.

That was the part she liked most.

She had seen it with technology.

The way his eyes changed when someone mentioned a difficult technical problem.

The way he leaned forward.

The way his replies became faster.

The way he stopped noticing everything around him.

It wasn't arrogance.

It was passion.

He loved understanding things.

And Sylvia had started wondering whether she could ever make him look at a person the way he looked at a problem he desperately wanted to solve.

Then she immediately felt embarrassed by her own thought.

What am I thinking?

She returned to her work.

---

That evening, Muse arrived home carrying two bags.

Sylvia looked up from the sofa.

> "What are those?"

> "Food."

She frowned.

> "You bought dinner?"

> "Yes."

> "You cooked yesterday."

> "I know."

> "Then why?"

> "I thought you might be tired."

She looked at him.

He placed the bags on the table.

> "I didn't know what you liked, so I got different things."

She blinked.

Different things.

He had bought three different dishes.

One was mild.

One was spicy.

One was something sweet.

She looked at him.

> "You remembered."

> "Remembered what?"

> "I said I like spicy food."

> "Yes."

> "And?"

> "You also said you don't like things too sweet."

Her expression softened.

> "You remembered that too."

I looked away.

> "You remembered mine."

She went quiet.

That sentence had slipped out.

I hadn't meant to say it like that.

She slowly smiled.

> "I suppose that's fair."

She opened one of the containers.

> "This one is spicy?"

> "Yes."

> "And this?"

> "Mild."

> "And this?"

> "Dessert."

She looked at the dessert.

Then at me.

> "You don't like very sweet things."

> "No."

> "Then why did you buy it?"

I paused.

> "You might."

She stared at me.

Then quietly smiled.

> "Maybe I do."

We sat down.

She tasted the spicy food.

Her eyes widened.

> "That's actually good."

> "I know."

> "You ate here before?"

> "No."

> "Then how did you know?"

I shrugged.

> "Guess."

She looked at me for a second.

Then smiled.

> "You're better at guessing than I thought."

I didn't answer.

She took another bite.

Then said,

> "You know what?"

> "What?"

> "You remember more than you think."

I looked at her.

> "What does that mean?"

> "Nothing."

She smiled.

> "Eat."

I did.

---

After dinner, I was working at the dining table.

Sylvia was reading something on the sofa.

The room was quiet.

Then she suddenly asked,

> "Muse?"

> "Hmm?"

> "What is your favourite color?"

I looked up.

> "Why?"

> "Just answer."

> "Black."

She smiled.

> "Of course."

> "What does that mean?"

> "Nothing."

> "You keep saying that."

> "Because you ask too many questions."

I looked at her.

> "Your turn."

> "My favorite color?"

> "Yes."

> "Blue."

> "Why?"

She smiled.

> "Because I like it."

> "That's not an explanation."

> "It doesn't need one."

I nodded.

> "Fair."

A few minutes passed.

Then she asked,

> "Favorite place?"

I thought about it.

> "Home."

She looked surprised.

> "Really?"

> "Yes."

> "Even though you travel?"

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

I looked at my laptop.

> "Quiet."

She smiled.

> "Anything else?"

I thought.

> "Depends on who's there."

The words came out before I could stop them.

Silence.

I looked up.

Sylvia was staring at me.

Her cheeks had turned slightly pink.

I immediately looked back at my laptop.

Why did I say that?

She quietly asked,

> "Who do you want there?"

I swallowed.

> "That was hypothetical."

She smiled.

> "Coward."

I looked at her.

She laughed.

> "I'm joking."

I went back to work.

But my mind was no longer on the code.

---

Later that night, Sylvia stood in the kitchen drinking water.

Muse walked past her.

She watched him.

He stopped.

> "What?"

> "You always drink water before sleeping."

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

> "Habit."

She smiled.

> "You really have a lot of habits."

> "Apparently."

She took another sip.

Then asked,

> "Do you remember my favorite color?"

> "Blue."

She smiled.

> "Good."

> "You told me."

> "I did."

> "You did."

She looked at him.

> "And yours?"

> "Black."

> "I remember."

He nodded.

Neither moved.

Then Sylvia said,

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "You know, you're not as unreadable as you think."

He looked at her.

> "I'm pretty unreadable."

She smiled.

> "No."

She stepped a little closer.

> "You're just quiet."

He stared at her.

> "And those are different?"

> "Very."

> "How?"

She smiled.

> "Quiet people still show things."

She walked away before he could answer.

Muse remained standing there.

For once...

He didn't know what to say.

Because somehow, that one sentence had reached somewhere he usually kept locked.

*Quiet people still show things.*

I looked toward the hallway where she had disappeared.

Maybe she was right.

Maybe I wasn't as unreadable as I thought.

Or maybe...

She was simply the first person who had bothered to look closely enough.

I smiled faintly.

Then turned off the kitchen light.

Tomorrow was another day.

Ten days after tomorrow?

No.

I mentally counted.

Ten days until the wedding preparations became serious.

Fourteen days since everything had changed.

Fourteen days until I was supposed to call her my wife.

For the first time, that word didn't feel strange anymore.

It felt like something waiting for me.

And somehow...

I was looking forward to it.

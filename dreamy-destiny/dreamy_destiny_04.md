# Chapter 4: Domestic Debugging

I thought living together would be simple.

It wasn't.

Not because Sylvia was difficult.

Actually, that was the problem.

She was too reasonable.

There was no obvious reason to argue with her.

And somehow that made everything harder.

The next morning, I walked into the kitchen and found her standing in front of the refrigerator.

She was staring inside it.

I stood behind her for a few seconds.

> "What are you looking for?"

She didn't turn around.

> "Something edible."

I looked at the refrigerator.

> "It is full."

> "Yes."

> "So?"

> "So most of it is not ready to eat."

I walked closer.

She moved aside.

> "Look."

I looked.

There were vegetables.

Milk.

Eggs.

Some containers.

Fruit.

And several things I didn't recognize.

> "This is food."

She looked at me.

> "I know."

> "Then what's the problem?"

> "You don't understand."

> "I probably don't."

She sighed.

> "I need to leave in forty minutes."

> "Oh."

> "And I don't have time to cook."

I looked at the containers.

Then at her.

> "There's bread."

She stared at me.

> "Bread?"

> "Yes."

> "For breakfast?"

> "Yes."

She narrowed her eyes.

> "You're a software engineer."

> "Yes."

> "You earn well."

> "Yes."

> "And this is your solution?"

I thought about it.

> "Efficient."

She burst out laughing.

I couldn't help smiling.

> "Fine."

She closed the refrigerator.

> "I'll make breakfast."

> "You don't have to."

> "I know."

That answer again.

I looked at her.

> "You say that a lot."

> "What?"

> "I know. You say it whenever I tell you that you don't have to do something."

She smiled.

> "Because you keep telling me I don't have to do things."

> "Because you don't."

> "Maybe I want to."

I went quiet.

There it was again.

Because I want to.

I looked away.

> "Okay."

She walked toward the kitchen counter.

> "You can help."

> "What should I do?"

> "Cut those."

She pointed toward some vegetables.

I picked up the knife.

She watched me.

> "Why are you holding it like that?"

> "Like what?"

> "Like you're about to perform surgery."

> "I am cutting vegetables."

> "You're making them afraid."

I looked at the vegetables.

> "They don't have feelings."

> "You don't know that."

I looked at her.

She was smiling.

> "Fine."

I relaxed my hand.

She nodded.

> "Better."

We continued silently for a while.

Then she glanced at me.

> "You really are comfortable with everything."

> "What do you mean?"

> "You don't ask many questions."

> "I ask when necessary."

> "And when is it necessary?"

> "When I need information."

She shook her head.

> "You make everything sound like work."

> "Maybe that's because I'm working."

She looked at me.

> "You're cutting carrots."

> "I am aware."

She laughed again.

I didn't know why, but I liked hearing it.

---

Breakfast was ready fifteen minutes later.

We sat across from each other.

Sylvia looked at my plate.

> "You're not eating enough."

> "I am."

> "No."

> "I am."

She pointed at my plate.

> "That is not enough."

I looked down.

> "It is enough for me."

> "You say that now."

She put another piece on my plate.

I looked at it.

Then at her.

> "I can eat."

> "I know."

> "You don't have to monitor me."

> "I know."

I smiled.

> "You're doing it anyway."

> "Yes."

I ate it.

She smiled.

> "Good."

For some reason, that tiny approval felt strangely satisfying.

This is getting dangerous.

I continued eating.

After breakfast, Sylvia stood up and began clearing the table.

I took the plates from her.

> "I'll do it."

She looked at me.

> "You sure?"

> "Yes."

> "Okay."

She handed me the remaining dishes.

I washed them.

She stood nearby for a moment.

Then asked,

> "Do you always clean immediately?"

> "Yes."

> "Why?"

> "Because leaving them there means I'll have to deal with them later."

She stared at me.

> "You really think like a programmer."

I glanced at her.

> "Is that bad?"

> "No."

She smiled.

> "It's cute."

My hand stopped under the running water.

I looked at her.

She seemed to realize what she'd said.

Her cheeks became slightly warm.

She immediately looked away.

> "I mean... organized."

I turned back to the sink.

> "Right."

*Cute.*

My brain repeated the word once.

Then again.

I ignored it.

---

We left for work separately.

Again.

But this time, before leaving, Sylvia stood near the door.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Tonight, we should talk."

> "About?"

> "Our apartment."

I looked around.

> "What's wrong with it?"

> "Nothing."

> "Then?"

> "We need some rules."

> "Rules?"

> "Yes."

I nodded.

> "Okay."

She smiled.

> "Don't look so serious."

> "I am listening."

> "You look like I'm about to give you a legal contract."

I almost smiled.

> "Go to work."

She pointed at me.

> "See? That's exactly what I mean."

> "What?"

> "You keep talking like that."

> "Like what?"

> "Like a manager."

> "I don't."

> "You do."

She opened the door.

> "See you tonight."

> "See you."

She stepped out.

Then turned around.

> "And don't work too late."

> "I won't."

She looked at me suspiciously.

> "You promise?"

> "Yes."

> "Good."

She left.

I closed the door.

Then stood there for a moment.

I think I'm being managed.

---

That evening, I returned slightly earlier than usual.

Sylvia was already home.

She was sitting on the sofa with her laptop.

She looked up.

> "You're early."

> "You told me not to work late."

She smiled.

> "You listened?"

> "Yes."

> "Interesting."

I put my bag down.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

She closed her laptop.

> "Come sit."

I sat opposite her.

She looked serious.

> "We need rules."

> "Okay."

> "First."

She raised one finger.

> "Neither of us has to ask permission to do anything."

I nodded.

> "Fair."

> "Second."

Another finger.

> "Both of us have work. So neither of us expects the other to always be available."

> "Agreed."

> "Third."

Another finger.

> "If something bothers you, say it."

I paused.

> "That seems obvious."

> "No."

She looked directly at me.

> "It isn't."

I didn't answer.

She continued.

> "You seem like the type who will notice something, decide it isn't important enough to mention, keep it inside, and then somehow remember it six months later."

I stared at her.

That was...

Uncomfortably accurate.

> "Maybe."

She smiled.

> "I knew it."

I leaned back.

> "Your turn."

> "My turn?"

> "You're making rules."

I thought for a second.

> "Fine."

I raised one finger.

> "Don't move my things."

She laughed.

> "That is your rule?"

> "Yes."

> "You only have one rule?"

> "Two."

She raised an eyebrow.

> "What's the second?"

> "Don't reorganize my desk."

She smiled.

> "Why?"

> "I know where everything is."

> "Your desk looks like a disaster."

> "It is an organized disaster."

She laughed.

> "Fine."

I nodded.

Then looked at her.

> "Anything else?"

She shook her head.

> "No."

> "Then we're done?"

> "Not exactly."

> "What else?"

She pointed toward the kitchen.

> "You left the cabinet open."

I looked.

The cabinet was open.

I frowned.

> "I didn't."

> "You did."

> "I closed it."

> "It is open."

I stood up.

She watched me.

I closed it.

> "There."

She smiled.

> "Good."

I sat down again.

Thirty seconds later, she pointed.

> "Your shoes."

I looked toward the entrance.

> "They're aligned."

> "They're not."

I stared at them.

They were, in fact, slightly misaligned.

I fixed them.

She smiled.

> "You're learning."

I looked at her.

> "Are these the rules?"

> "No."

> "Then what are they?"

> "I'm improving you."

I laughed.

> "You're impossible."

> "And yet you're marrying me."

I went silent.

She went silent too.

That sentence had slipped out so naturally that neither of us had been prepared for it.

She looked down.

I looked at her.

Then I smiled.

> "Apparently."

She smiled back.

> "Apparently..."

---

Later that night, I was working on my laptop in the living room.

Sylvia had gone to take a shower.

I noticed the Wi-Fi router blinking strangely.

I frowned.

I checked it.

The connection was unstable.

I moved closer.

Restarted it.

Nothing.

I opened the settings.

A configuration was wrong.

I fixed it.

The connection stabilized.

I closed the laptop.

Simple.

Then I noticed the loose cable behind the cabinet.

I crouched down.

The connector was damaged.

I found a spare.

Replaced it.

Secured the cable.

Done.

I stood up.

> "Much better."

I heard footsteps.

Sylvia entered the room.

She stopped.

> "What are you doing?"

> "Nothing."

She looked at the router.

Then the cable.

Then me.

> "You fixed it."

> "It was loose."

> "You didn't tell me."

> "You were busy."

She walked closer.

> "You fixed the router, organized the cable and probably cleaned half the mess behind there."

I looked away.

> "It was annoying."

She smiled.

> "You secretly fix things."

> "No."

> "You do."

> "I just don't like broken things."

She looked at me for a moment.

Then quietly said,

> "I think I like that."

I looked at her.

I smiled.

> "Don't get used to compliments."

> "I wasn't."

> "Good."

She walked toward the sofa.

I followed.

For some reason, I kept thinking about what she had said.

*I think I like that.*

A stupid sentence.

A simple sentence.

Yet somehow it stayed with me.

We sat together.

She opened her laptop.

I opened mine.

For the next half hour, neither of us spoke.

Just two people working in the same room.

It was oddly peaceful.

I glanced at her once.

She was focused on her screen, hair falling slightly over her face.

I looked away.

A few minutes later, she spoke without looking at me.

> "You looked."

I froze.

> "What?"

> "You were looking at me."

> "I wasn't."

> "You were."

> "I was looking at the screen."

> "My screen?"

> "No."

She turned toward me.

I looked at her.

She smiled.

> "You're a terrible liar."

> "I wasn't lying."

> "You're doing it again."

> "What?"

> "Pretending nothing happened."

I looked back at my laptop.

> "Maybe I was just checking something."

> "What?"

I paused.

Then said the first thing that came to mind.

> "Your laptop."

She looked at hers.

> "Why?"

> "I don't know."

She laughed.

> "Exactly."

I smiled despite myself.

Then returned to my work.

Neither of us mentioned it again.

But the room felt a little warmer after that.

---

Before sleeping, Sylvia stood near the hallway.

> "Muse."

> "Yeah?"

> "Tomorrow morning, don't make bread."

I raised an eyebrow.

> "Why?"

> "I'll make breakfast."

> "You don't have to."

She smiled.

> "I know."

She walked away.

I watched her disappear into her room.

Then quietly said to myself,

> "Good."

I wasn't sure whether I meant the breakfast.

Or her.

I decided not to investigate.

Some things were better left unsolved.

At least for tonight.

# Chapter 7: Her Side of the City

The next morning started differently.

Not dramatically.

Nothing had changed.

The same apartment.

The same kitchen.

The same sunlight slipping through the curtains.

But Sylvia was already dressed when I came out.

She was standing in front of the mirror, adjusting an earring.

I stopped for a moment.

She noticed me through the mirror.

> "Good morning."

> "Morning."

> "You're staring."

> "I wasn't."

> "You were."

I walked toward the kitchen.

> "I was looking at the mirror."

She smiled.

> "Of course."

I started making coffee.

Then she said,

> "Today you are coming with me."

I looked back.

> "Where?"

> "My office."

> "Why?"

> "Because I have a client meeting."

I paused.

> "And?"

> "And you're attending."

> "Why?"

She turned around.

> "Because your company is involved."

That made sense.

A joint project.

Different companies.

Same city.

Same deal.

And apparently, now the same apartment.

> "Okay."

She smiled.

> "That's it?"

> "What else should I say?"

> "I don't know."

She picked up her bag.

> "Maybe ask what I'm wearing."

I looked at her.

She was wearing a formal blazer, neatly styled hair, and the kind of expression that made it obvious she was already mentally inside her workday.

> "You look professional."

She smiled.

> "Thank you."

Then she walked past me.

After two steps, she stopped.

> "Wait."

> "What?"

> "You didn't say I looked good."

I stared at her.

She raised an eyebrow.

> "Well?"

I looked at her properly.

> "You look good."

Her expression softened.

> "Better."

She walked toward the door.

I followed.

That was dangerously close to flirting.

I decided not to think about it.

---

Her office was nothing like mine.

Mine was quieter.

More screens.

More people staring at code.

Less talking.

Her office had movement everywhere.

People discussing presentations.

Phones ringing.

Someone arguing about numbers.

Someone else rushing toward a meeting room with a stack of documents.

And somehow Sylvia fit perfectly into all of it.

The moment she entered, her posture changed.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

Her steps became more purposeful.

Her voice became sharper.

Her expressions became controlled.

People greeted her.

She responded to everyone.

Then one of her colleagues approached.

> "Sylvia, the revised deck is ready."

> "Good. Did you fix slide twelve?"

> "Yes."

> "Not just the numbers?"

> "The wording too."

> "Perfect."

She turned toward me.

> "This is Muse."

Her colleague smiled.

> "Oh."

I raised an eyebrow.

She looked at me.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

Her colleague laughed.

> "I've heard about you."

I looked at Sylvia.

She immediately said,

> "Only professional things."

Her colleague smiled.

> "Mostly."

Sylvia gave her a look.

> "Go."

> "Okay, okay."

She left.

I looked at Sylvia.

> "Mostly?"

She ignored me.

> "Meeting room."

I followed.

---

The meeting began ten minutes later.

I had attended hundreds of meetings.

This one was different.

Because I was watching her.

Not as the woman who made breakfast.

Not as the woman who argued about cabinet doors.

Not as the woman who shared ice cream with me.

She was working.

And she was good.

Very good.

A client raised a concern.

Sylvia listened without interrupting.

She took a note.

Then asked three questions.

The first clarified the problem.

The second exposed the actual concern.

The third changed the entire direction of the discussion.

The room became quiet.

She explained her recommendation calmly.

No unnecessary words.

No hesitation.

No attempt to dominate anyone.

Just confidence.

I leaned back.

So this is Sylvia.

I had seen her work before.

But never this closely.

A senior manager disagreed with her.

She didn't become defensive.

She simply said,

> "I understand your concern. But if we change the strategy based on that assumption, we will solve the wrong problem."

The manager paused.

> "Why?"

She turned the screen toward him.

> "Because the data doesn't support it."

She showed him the numbers.

He looked at them.

Then nodded.

> "Fair."

Sylvia continued.

I looked at her.

There was something oddly satisfying about watching someone be exactly where they belonged.

She wasn't pretending to be confident.

She was confident.

And for some reason...

I felt proud.

Why am I proud?

I looked down at my notes.

That was a strange feeling.

I barely knew her.

And yet I was sitting there thinking,

She's incredible.

---

After the meeting, we walked out.

She looked at me.

> "You were quiet."

> "I was listening."

> "You always listen."

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "What did you think?"

> "About?"

> "My presentation."

I looked at her.

> "It was good."

She frowned.

> "Good?"

> "Yes."

> "That is your review?"

I thought about it.

> "You handled the disagreement well."

Her expression changed slightly.

> "How?"

> "You didn't argue with him."

> "I didn't need to."

> "You made him arrive at the conclusion himself."

She smiled.

> "That's the point."

> "I know."

She looked at me for a moment.

Then asked,

> "You actually paid attention."

> "Yes."

> "I thought you were bored."

> "No."

She smiled.

> "Good."

We continued walking.

Then she suddenly asked,

> "Were you proud?"

I stopped.

She stopped too.

> "What?"

> "When I handled the meeting."

I looked at her.

She was watching me carefully.

I didn't know why the question made me uncomfortable.

Maybe because the answer was too obvious.

> "Yes."

Her eyes softened.

> "Why?"

I looked away.

> "Because you were good."

She smiled.

> "That's all?"

> "Yes."

She didn't believe me.

But she didn't push.

Instead, she simply said,

> "Thank you."

And somehow that felt heavier than it should have.

---

We had lunch together.

Her colleagues joined us.

I mostly listened.

They talked about Sylvia.

Not gossip.

Just little stories.

How she once stayed late to fix a client's presentation.

How she had apparently argued with a senior partner over an incorrect analysis.

How she remembered everyone's birthdays.

How she could tell when someone was lying in a meeting.

I looked at her.

> "You do all that?"

She shrugged.

> "It's my job."

One colleague laughed.

> "No, it isn't."

Sylvia looked at her.

> "What?"

> "You care too much."

Sylvia rolled her eyes.

> "Someone has to."

I smiled.

Her colleague looked at me.

> "Good luck."

I frowned.

> "With what?"

She smiled.

> "With her."

Sylvia immediately said,

> "Don't scare him."

> "I am warning him."

I looked at Sylvia.

> "I think I'll survive."

She smiled.

> "See?"

Her colleague laughed.

> "You two are already acting married."

Both of us went quiet.

Then Sylvia took a sip of water.

I looked at my plate.

> "Already?"

The word stayed there.

---

After lunch, Sylvia had another meeting.

I had work of my own.

So we separated.

She went one way.

I went another.

Halfway down the corridor, I looked back.

She was already speaking to someone, completely absorbed in work.

She didn't notice me.

I smiled faintly.

Then kept walking.

---

My own meeting was straightforward.

Technical architecture.

Three engineers.

Two managers.

One disagreement.

Normal.

I explained my position.

Someone challenged it.

We discussed it.

Eventually, we reached a solution.

But for some reason, my mind kept drifting.

Not toward the architecture.

Toward her.

The way she had handled the meeting.

The way she spoke.

The way people trusted her.

The way she didn't need anyone's approval.

And that smile when I told her I was proud.

I looked at the screen.

Focus.

I did.

For approximately seven minutes.

Then my phone vibrated.

**Sylvia:** *How's your meeting?*

I smiled.

**Me:** *Fine.*

**Sylvia:** *Only fine?*

**Me:** *Good.*

**Sylvia:** *Better.*

I laughed quietly.

My teammate looked at me.

> "What?"

> "Nothing."

> "Her again?"

I ignored him.

He smiled.

> "Definitely her."

---

We met outside the building in the evening.

Sylvia was tired.

It was visible this time.

Her shoulders were slightly lower.

Her voice was softer.

I looked at her.

> "Long day?"

> "Yes."

> "You hungry?"

> "Yes."

> "Let's eat."

She looked at me.

> "Where?"

> "You choose."

She smiled.

> "You always let me choose."

> "I don't mind."

> "That's dangerous."

> "Why?"

> "One day I'll choose somewhere expensive."

> "Okay."

She stared at me.

> "That's it?"

> "Yes."

She laughed.

> "Fine."

We started walking.

After a few steps, she quietly said,

> "You were different today."

> "How?"

> "You watched me."

I looked at her.

> "I was there."

> "No."

She shook her head.

> "You were actually watching."

I didn't answer.

She smiled.

> "I liked it."

I looked ahead.

My face stayed neutral.

Inside, however, something had gone completely wrong.

Because I liked hearing that.

Far more than I should have.

---

We eventually stopped at a small restaurant.

Nothing fancy.

We ordered dinner.

Sylvia talked about her day.

I listened.

Halfway through, she stopped.

> "What?"

> "You're smiling."

> "I am?"

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "Again."

I looked down.

Maybe I was.

I didn't correct her.

She didn't tease me this time.

She simply continued talking.

And I realized something.

I had spent most of my life making sure nobody could easily understand what I felt.

I didn't mind it.

It protected things that belonged only to me.

But with Sylvia...

I wasn't sure I wanted to hide everything anymore.

That thought frightened me slightly.

Not enough to run away.

Just enough to make me notice it.

---

On the way home, Sylvia walked beside me.

Our hands occasionally came close.

Neither of us moved away.

Then, at one point, the back of her hand lightly brushed mine.

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

Neither said anything.

We continued walking.

A few seconds later, she did it again.

This time, I didn't move my hand away.

She smiled.

Very slightly.

I pretended not to notice.

But I did.

Of course I did.

I noticed everything about her now.

Her footsteps.

Her voice.

The way she smiled when she was trying not to.

The way she became quieter when she was embarrassed.

The way she looked at me before saying something important.

And perhaps that was the real problem.

I was starting to notice her too.

Not because she was my fiancée.

Not because we were going to get married.

Not because everyone had apparently already decided that we belonged together.

But simply because...

She was Sylvia.

And somehow, that was becoming enough.

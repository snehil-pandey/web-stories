# Chapter 10: What Do You Actually Like?

Saturday arrived without either of us realizing how quickly the week had passed.

There was no office.

No meetings.

No deadlines.

Just the two of us in the apartment.

Which, somehow, felt more dangerous.

Sylvia was sitting on the sofa with her laptop.

I was at the dining table working.

For almost an hour, neither of us said anything.

Then—

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "What do you like?"

I looked up.

> "Food?"

She stared at me.

> "Anything else?"

I thought about it.

> "Sleep."

She closed her laptop.

> "I am being serious."

> "So am I."

She gave me a look.

> "Fine. What do you like doing?"

> "Coding."

> "That's your work."

> "I enjoy it."

> "Okay."

She leaned forward.

> "Favorite movie?"

> "I don't know."

> "Song?"

> "I don't know."

> "Color?"

> "Black."

She frowned.

> "That's suspiciously convenient."

> "It's a color."

> "What about your favorite food?"

> "Depends."

> "On?"

> "What I'm hungry for."

She stared at me.

> "You're impossible."

I smiled.

> "You asked."

She crossed her arms.

> "Fine. Favorite place?"

> "Here."

Her expression changed.

That answer had somehow slipped out naturally.

She smiled.

> "That's actually cute."

> "It's practical."

> "Of course."

She opened her laptop again.

Then looked up.

> "Favorite person?"

I froze.

She waited.

I looked at her.

> "That's a strange question."

> "Why?"

> "Because you know the answer."

She smiled.

> "Do I?"

> "Yes."

> "Tell me."

I looked down.

Dangerous.

I could answer.

But I didn't want to.

Not yet.

So I said,

> "My mother."

She laughed.

> "Coward."

> "I answered."
> "Technically."

---

She wasn't going to stop.

I knew that.

She closed her laptop again.

> "You know what's funny?"

> "What?"

> "I know your professional history."

I looked at her.

> "I know what you studied."

She started counting on her fingers.

> "I know what projects you've worked on. I know your technical skills. I know which languages you use. I know your salary range. I know that your colleagues trust you."

I stared at her.

> "How do you know all that?"

She smiled innocently.

> "I'm a consultant."

> "That doesn't answer my question."

> "I have my methods."

> "That sounds suspicious."

She laughed.

> "But there's one thing I don't know."

> "What?"

She looked directly at me.

> "What you actually like."

I didn't answer.

She continued.

> "What makes you happy?"

Silence.

> "What annoys you?"

Silence.

> "What kind of person do you like?"

I looked away.

She leaned closer.

> "What kind of girl?"

I remained quiet.

She smiled.

> "See?"

> "What?"

> "You have an answer."

> "Maybe."

> "You're hiding it."

> "Maybe."

> "Why?"

I looked at her.

> "Because it's mine."

She became quiet.

For once, she didn't immediately tease me.

I continued,

> "Some things don't need to be public."

She nodded slowly.

> "I understand."

Then she smiled.

> "But I'm your fiancée."

I looked at her.

> "So?"

> "So I think I deserve access."

I laughed.

> "That isn't how privacy works."

She smiled.

> "Then I'll earn access."

Something about the way she said it made my chest tighten.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

---

Later, we went grocery shopping.

It should have been ordinary.

It wasn't.

Sylvia pushed the cart.

I walked beside her.

She picked up a packet.

> "Do you like this?"

> "Yes."

She put it in the cart.

Then another.

> "This?"

> "Yes."

Another.

> "This?"

> "Yes."

She suddenly stopped.

> "You're saying yes to everything."

> "Because I like them."

She looked at the cart.

> "Then why did you never buy them before?"

I looked at the shelves.

> "I forgot."

She stared at me.

> "You forgot what you liked?"

> "Sometimes."

She shook her head.

> "You're unbelievable."

We continued.

Then she stopped in front of a shelf.

> "Chocolate?"

> "Yes."

> "What kind?"

I pointed.

> "That one."

She picked it up.

Then looked at me.

> "You have a favorite."

> "Yes."

> "Why didn't you tell me?"

> "You didn't ask."

She stared.

> "I literally asked you ten minutes ago."

> "You asked about chocolate now."

She sighed.

> "Fine."

She put two bars into the cart.

> "One for you."

> "Why two?"

> "One for me."

I looked at her.

> "You don't like that one."

She smiled.

> "I know."

> "Then?"

> "I want to try yours."

I didn't know why that sentence felt strangely intimate.

Maybe because she said it so casually.

---

On the way back, she asked,

> "What about music?"

> "I listen to instrumental music while coding."

> "That isn't music taste."

> "It is."

> "What about when you're not coding?"

I thought.

> "Depends on my mood."

> "What mood are you usually in?"

> "Quiet."

She smiled.

> "I know."

We reached the apartment.

She took the grocery bags from me.

> "I'll cook."

> "You're tired."

> "No."

> "You worked all week."

> "So did you."

> "I can cook."

She looked at me.

> "You can?"

> "Yes."

> "Since when?"

> "Since always."

She laughed.

> "Why have you never cooked?"

> "You never asked."

She pointed at me.

> "You keep doing that."

> "Doing what?"

> "Giving me answers that technically make sense but emotionally make no sense."

I smiled.

> "That's because I'm a software engineer."

She laughed.

> "That's not an excuse."

---

She cooked.

I helped.

Mostly.

At one point, she was cutting vegetables while I stood beside her.

> "Why are you staring?"

> "I am not."

> "You are."

> "I was thinking."

> "About?"

> "Nothing."

She smiled.

> "You always say that when it's something."

I didn't answer.

She went back to cutting.

After a moment, I quietly said,

> "I like watching you cook."

Her knife stopped.

She looked at me.

> "What?"

I realized what I'd said.

> "Nothing."

> "No."

She smiled.

> "You said something."

I looked away.

> "I said I like watching you cook."

She became strangely quiet.

Then smiled.

> "Okay."

I continued helping.

But her smile stayed there.

---

That evening, we sat together after dinner.

She opened the chocolate.

The one she had bought.

My favorite.

She broke off a piece.

Then held it toward me.

> "Here."

I looked at her.

> "I can eat it myself."

> "I know."

> "Then?"

> "Take it."

I leaned forward.

She fed me the chocolate.

For some reason, neither of us laughed.

It was such a small thing.

Yet my heart reacted as though something important had happened.

She pulled her hand back.

> "Good?"

> "Yes."

She smiled.

> "Good."

I looked at the chocolate in her hand.

Then at her.

> "Why are you doing this?"

> "Doing what?"

> "Taking care of me."

She thought for a moment.

> "Because I want to."

That answer was simple.

Too simple.

I looked down.

Because I want to.

Maybe that was the kind of answer I had been avoiding.

The kind without logic.

Without calculations.

Without a safe explanation.

---

Later that night, Sylvia stood near the balcony.

I joined her.

The city was quiet.

Not silent.

Just distant.

She leaned against the railing.

> "Muse."

> "Hmm?"

> "Do you think we are moving too fast?"

I looked at her.

> "Marriage?"

She nodded.

I thought for a while.

> "Maybe."

Her eyes lowered.

Then I continued.

> "But I don't feel rushed."

She looked at me.

> "Why?"

> "Because every day with you feels normal."

She smiled faintly.

> "Normal?"

> "Yes."

> "That's romantic."

> "I wasn't trying to be."

> "I know."

She looked back at the city.

Then quietly said,

> "I've never felt this comfortable with someone."

I looked at her.

She continued,

> "Even when I'm nervous."

> "You're nervous around me?"

She laughed softly.

> "Sometimes."

> "Why?"

She looked at me.

> "Because I care what you think."

That answer stayed between us.

I didn't know what to say.

So I told her the truth.

> "I care what you think too."

She smiled.

> "About me?"

> "Yes."

> "Good."

She looked away.

For a while, we stood there together.

No confession.

No dramatic moment.

Nothing obvious.

Just two people standing beneath the same night sky, slowly discovering that the distance between them was becoming smaller.

And somewhere inside me, something had changed.

I still didn't know exactly what I liked.

But I was beginning to know one thing I didn't want to lose her.

I didn't say it.

Not yet.

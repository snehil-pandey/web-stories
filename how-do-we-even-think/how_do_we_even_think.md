# How Do We Even Think?

Once, there was a boy the world seemed to forget.

No warm greetings, no worried glances, just a hollow space where care should've been.

But forget all that for now. Let's meet our peculiar little protagonist, Muse.

Muse was... complicated.

But we won't waste breath painting his personality in adjectives.

Let's tumble straight into the moment.

---

It was a day drenched in stillness, the kind of peace that feels like the world's holding its breath.

And there lay Muse, sprawled on the cold floor like a discarded thought, limbs limp, soul heavier than he cared to admit.

His eyes clung to the ceiling, wide and unblinking, as if it held cosmic truths and ancient riddles meant only for him.

> "How do we even think?"

he whispered, not really asking anyone just casting the question into the void.

His voice was soft, almost swallowed by the silence, but heavy with something raw... a flicker of wonder, or maybe the ache of being too human.

For a brief moment, he imagined the ceiling blinking back, just once, to let him know he wasn't entirely alone.

---

And you know what?

The universe gave him a surprise.

Suddenly, the world bent.

He felt himself rising or rather, zooming while his body remained still on the floor, completely in awe.

Yet his mind lifted, like a thread being pulled through the layers of existence.

It was more absurd than anything he could imagine.

The ceiling was no longer a ceiling.

It became a canvas, then a membrane, then a net of glistening chains.

He rushed into them. His breath slowed.

Time thinned. Muse's eyes widened as the surface blurred, twisted, and began to pull him in.

Not physically, not entirely, but his mind had started to travel.

First, he floated through molecular chains like beaded strings unraveling before him.

Then atoms, swirling systems, each a miniature universe.

He plunged into the orbit of electrons, slipping through orbitals like Saturn's rings, only these were made of silence.

And then... the nucleus.

It beat. Yes, it was beating, like a heart.

Each thump sent Muse deeper past quarks, past strings, past the very rules themselves.

Suddenly, he wasn't inside anything anymore.

---

He... he was outside.

What? Outside... floating amid galaxies billions of them, thin, shining, twinkling like stars on a moonless night.

The starlike scatter began to form a pattern clearer and clearer the closer he drifted to the center.

The galaxies were arranging themselves... into a webbed structure.

The cosmos pulsed. It wasn't random. It meant something.

It was speaking not in words, but in rhythm, in light, in patterns older than thought itself. Muse could feel it the universe wasn't just alive, it was trying to explain something. To show him.

The structure around him shimmered with eerie familiarity, like a dream half-remembered from a life he never lived.

It was right there on the edge of recognition, something he felt he should know, something buried deep in the folds of memory he never made.

Muse felt it, that strange tightening in the chest before a revelation. He was close. Something was about to happen. And then...

He drifted toward the core, a luminous vortex at the heart of it all, swirling like liquid thought. It glowed with impossible colors and was wrapped in whispers... not in any language, but made of raw intuition.

They didn't speak to him. They unfolded inside him, like memories blooming from silence.

---

Then came the visions of:

A child, hunched in the dark, scribbling with furious grace.

The words bled onto the paper, twitching, alive, coiling like thoughts that never wanted to be born.

But inside the story... an old man sat in a room full of mirrors.

Only... the mirrors didn't reflect him.

Each pane held a different version one weeping, one laughing like madness itself, another kneeling in prayer, one screaming without sound, and one simply... staring back, with hollow eyes and a grin that knew too much.

The man flung, pleading with his own reflections, justifying things no one asked about, as if guilt needed to be proven innocent.

The mirrors began to melt, refract, shatter, multiply glass and identity blending into one another.

The walls folded in, or maybe they stretched outward, infinitely. Time twisted briefly.

It was all unraveling.

It felt like memory, it felt like prophecy, it felt like a joke whispered by the void to itself.

Muse trembled or maybe he didn't. Maybe he was the child.

Maybe he was the mirrors. Maybe he wasn't at all.

And amid the cacophony, the flash-flood of color and feeling and noise that wasn't noise, Muse asked, at a whim or maybe it was the universe using his voice:

> "Why does this even exist?"

A sudden calm voice, one that sounded like Muse himself, but older, wiser... or maybe broken, spoke:

> "Thought is the illusion of control... the echo of a question... trying to survive."

He felt it, the answer. It was close.

It wasn't a word, it was a pattern. A feeling. A connection. 

The origin of thought wasn't inside the mind... it was outside, a broadcast, a signal.

Thought wasn't being created. It was being received.

The core opened. The answer unfolded. It still wasn't a word. It was a vibration. A pulse shaped like understanding.

Muse reached out...

Fingertips brushing the lattice of everything...

The universe inhale...

---

> SMACK!

---

A crack. Not sound. Not light. Not time.

Just... something broke. Everything shattered. 

Stars tore like wet paper. Galaxies collapsed mid-breath. 

The cosmos didn't fade, it jerked out of existence.

Muse blinked, and it was gone.

No warning.

No meaning.

No center to hold onto.

Only the ghost of a feeling he couldn't name, vibrating where vision used to be.

And in its place:

> "Stop daydreaming like a lunatic. I've called you three times."

Muse sat up.

Not falling. Not floating.

Just there.

Floor under skin. Light too bright. 

The ceiling, ordinary again. Too ordinary. Fan spinning, but slower than memory allows.

A scent, burnt edges of dinner or dust or both. Somewhere, a sound: a clock tick? a voice behind a wall? the hum of an annoyed world pretending it never paused?

---

Muse blinked. Once. Twice.

Was it morning? Was it now?

And above, the ceiling stared back.

Plain. Silent. Watching.

His mother stood before him, a slipper in one hand. No patience in her eyes. No mercy in her stance. 

The dream was gone. No, ripped away. Torn like wet cloth, leaving no edge to grasp.

But Muse's fingers trembled. Still... As if they'd brushed the pulse of something vast...

Something true.

---

*Some thoughts arrive without a sound.*  
*And some never leave.*
